How to send photos to an insurance adjuster

so they actually open the file

The desk will not click your Dropbox.

They will not scroll a 130-photo gallery.

They will not guess that the blurry wide shot of the living room is the cracked lamp.

They want one PDF. Labeled. In order. Small enough to email.

This page is the ritual. The tool under it builds that PDF in your browser. Nothing is uploaded.

What gets the file bounced

  • “Wish we could use Dropbox or album links, but most won’t allow it.”
  • “Ended up in the 130-picture range — had to split it into several PDFs.”
  • “For God’s sake label your photos. Don’t expect someone to know what you’re trying to show. If it’s a roof, label it as ‘north slope, shingles missing.’”
  • “No, I didn’t see the cracked lamp in the photo of your entire living room.”
  • “The photos that were uploaded aren’t properly labeled or associated with the rooms in which they were taken.”

If you already have CompanyCam, JobNimbus, AccuLynx, or a shared drive: good. That is capture. This page is the packet the other office files.

The ritual

  1. Sequence, don’t dump. Roof: elevations first, then each slope, then close-ups, then interiors if they matter. Water/interior: address/exterior, then room by room, then the defect close-up. Progress: before, during, after — same angle if you can.
  2. One caption that names the place and the fact. Not IMG_4402. Yes: North slope — 4 missing shingles at hip. Yes: Kitchen ceiling — 12-inch stain above sink, no active drip.
  3. Attachment, not a link. One PDF. If the portal caps size, two PDFs split by elevation or floor — still labeled. Not a gallery URL.
  4. Leave the junk out. Thumb, truck bumper, third identical wide shot.
  5. You stay the editor. This page does not pick “the photos that win the claim.”

What the PDF looks like

Cover line: address, date, claim or job number if you typed them. Photos in your order. Caption under each frame. Small enough to email. No watermark.
